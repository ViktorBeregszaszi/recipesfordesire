The workshop

Desire can be hard to talk about, even with the person you love

Many couples carry fantasies they have never said out loud. Keeping quiet can feel safer than risking an awkward silence, even years into the relationship.

Recipes for Desire is a card game we designed to make these conversations easy, playful and deeply connecting. In our workshop, couples find the desires they share and grow together towards what brings them aliveness.

It fits the slot in your programme for couples who want to deepen their connection, from newcomers to tantra to experienced practitioners, and for couples and flirts of any orientation.

What couples experience

Four steps towards a sexy date idea, through a metaphor

Creating a fantasy with someone you love is a little like cooking a meal for them. You find out what they love, leave out what they cannot eat, and put your heart into the dish.

  1. Arrive

    We start by setting intentions and taking three deep breaths together. Then a playful round of thumbs up or down to different foods you like helps you notice your own yes and no, before anything more personal comes up.

  2. Remove the allergies

    Each of you gets a deck of fantasy cards, each card one ingredient for a sexy date: a place, an activity, a tool. Whatever is not for you gets discarded. A no needs no explanation.

  3. Find your favourites, season with maybes

    Together you discover the cards you both said hell yes to. The maybes become gentle conversations about what would make them a yes.

  4. Cook your recipe

    You combine your favourites into a date idea with a starter, a main course and a dessert. You describe it to each other in vivid detail, then write it into your Recipe Book to take home.

Why it works

A clear structure lets couples think less, feel more and speak from the heart

Out of the head, into the senses

In tantra, presence comes first. Talking about desire usually pulls people into their heads, searching for the right words and wondering how they will land. In the game, the questions and the steps are printed on the cards, freeing couples to slow down, listen and feel.

Why we cook

Desire is a tender subject, and a metaphor gives it a little distance. “That ingredient is not for me” is easier to say, and to hear, than turning down a partner’s fantasy. And everyone already knows how to talk about food: allergies, favourite flavours, the dish you have always wanted to try. In the kitchen, people find their words and their imagination.

Get familiar with your yeses and nos

In an intimate moment, the wish to please or the rush of excitement can blur a no, and some people only feel it afterwards. The game gives couples time with a fantasy before it happens. They feel into it together, negotiate and align. That helps them stay within their boundaries, and it can also show them a yes they had never let themselves explore.

Who we are

A game designer and a storyteller, who made this game for themselves first

It started with a book. We were reading about intimate fantasies, and at the back was a list of desires to mark yes, no or maybe.

But the list was boringly static, so Viktor grabbed a pad of post-it notes and turned it into cards. Suddenly we could move the ideas around, combine them and play. That evening became the first game of Recipes for Desire, and it kept us talking for a whole night. It was vulnerable and beautiful, and it brought us closer.

We want couples to keep their relationship exciting, years in. The game is also practice for something bigger. Recognising your feelings and boundaries, saying what you want truthfully, and listening to others with curiosity and compassion are skills that will make this world a better place to live in for all.

How we hold the room

We connect through words

Recipes for Desire is a conversation workshop. There is no nudity, intimate touch or sexual activity.

Two facilitators are present the whole time, and Rebecca is there for anyone who needs support. We work alongside your festival’s own support team. The framework draws on Nonviolent Communication, Authentic Relating and boundary work.
